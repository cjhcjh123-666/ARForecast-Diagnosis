## Main findings

1. **Cross-family structure transfer (10/10).** With family-label supervision on the 270 clean primitive windows, pretrained beats matched random on balanced OOD compositional routing for every base LM tested (+7.5 to +31.4pp), and on the second OOD set (`bal3n`) too. Clean recognition and the shuffle control also favour pretrained for all models, i.e. the effect is not a Qwen artefact and is not explained by marginal value statistics (shuffling the 64 time positions destroys most of the advantage).
2. **The conversion to a decision is interface-dependent (10/10 flip).** Re-labelling the *same* clean windows with the realised-future best expert (`oracle-label`) turns the routing advantage negative for all ten models (−5.6 to −21.4pp). Family↔oracle agreement on clean windows is only ≈64%. Interpretation: pretraining organises a stable latent partition, not a realisation-level expert choice.
3. **The effect survives on real data (10/10 models).** Zero-shot routers (trained only on synthetic clean primitives) beat matched random controls on 10–15 of 15 datasets per model, with BH-FDR significance on most; median relative routed-MSE change from -28% to -6%. Pooled raw MSE across datasets is dominated by m4_hourly, so relative per-dataset changes are the reportable quantity. The honest caveat: against the hand-crafted temporal-feature router the same models only win 8–11/15 with median ≈0 to −4%, i.e. the real-data result establishes an advantage over random initialisation, not over an engineered feature baseline.
4. **Structure ≠ numerics.** The frozen numerical read-out and native generation tell a different story: the read-out advantage is model-dependent, and direct greedy generation is 4–15× worse than the oracle expert and *worse than the best fixed expert* for every local base LM. Matched random models never emit a parseable forecast (0.00 parse rate), so pretraining buys surface number formatting, not forecasting accuracy.

5. **The advantage is not a geometry artefact.** Randomly projecting every model down to a common width keeps the pretrained−random gap positive at every width tested (64–2048 dims), so the fact that Qwen carries 4096-d states while the TSFMs carry 384–1280-d states does not explain the cross-architecture difference. PCA fitted on the 270 training windows is much harsher (it is rank-limited by the training set), and the gap shrinks there as well — reported as such in `dimension_matching.csv`.
6. **The gap is not a linear-accessibility artefact and not an expert-bank artefact.** Replacing the linear router with a 2-layer MLP64 keeps the sign for every model, and enlarging the expert bank from 3 to 5 experts keeps the advantage positive for every model for which that sensitivity was run — it shrinks roughly by half, because the 5-way decision is genuinely harder.
7. **Scale is non-monotone and the headline is seed-stable.** Qwen3 0.6B +0.175, 1.7B +0.158, 8B +0.311 (3 seeds; the two small models overlap, only 8B separates), and the Qwen3-8B headline stays positive on all 10 seeds (+0.277 [0.253,0.304] on bal3, +0.267 [0.253,0.281] on bal3n).

## Negative findings (retained, not hidden)

- Oracle-label supervision is uniformly worse for pretrained than random (see above) — the opposite sign of the family-label result.
- Time-series foundation models do **not** reproduce the LM-style transfer gain: Chronos-T5-base +0.11, Chronos-T5-small/Bolt ≈0, TimesFM −0.05, MOMENT −0.03, Moirai −0.06 (family-label, bal3).
- API-served chat models are worse at direct numerical forecasting than local base LMs: strict parse rate ≤33% (llama-3.1-70b), most 0–6%; parsed windows are 2.5–5.8× the best-fixed expert.
- Native generation for Gemma/DeepSeek needed environment fixes (see blockers doc); nothing was re-run to improve a number, and both crash logs are kept.

## Reviewer risks remaining

- **Multiplicity.** CIs are nominal 95% intervals (seed-level for the 3-seed models, window-level for the real-world table); only the real-world table carries BH-FDR q-values. No correction is applied across models.
- **One protocol.** Everything is measured under one synthetic generator, one expert bank family, one representation point (final layer) and C=64/H=16; the real-world section is strict zero-shot routing, not a forecasting benchmark.
- **Pooling and 10-seed coverage.** Pooling (last vs mean) is measured for Qwen3-8B only; the 10-seed headline exists for Qwen3-8B only (the other models have 3 seeds). The MLP-router check now covers all models.
- **Real-data baseline.** Against the hand-crafted temporal-feature router the LM advantage is not established (4–11/15 datasets), which must be stated alongside the random-control win.

## Recommended claim

> Under our controlled protocol, weights obtained by language-model pretraining support a linearly readable, cross-family temporal structure that transfers to unseen temporal compositions and to real data, while the *magnitude* of the numerical benefit depends entirely on the downstream interface: it is positive for a stable structural partition, negative for realised-future expert labels, and absent for direct numerical generation.

This is Case B/C in the pre-registered decision tree: the effect is a recurring cross-family phenomenon (Case A direction) but its conversion into forecasting utility is interface-dependent (Case C), so the paper must not claim "language pretraining universally improves forecasting".

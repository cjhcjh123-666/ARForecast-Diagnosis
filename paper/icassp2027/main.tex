\title{RECOGNIZE, DON'T GENERATE: LANGUAGE PRETRAINING AS A TEMPORAL-STRUCTURE

\begin{abstract}
Whether language pretraining contributes to LLM-based time-series forecasting
beyond architecture and tokenizer capacity remains contested.  Under a strict
control (identical Qwen3-8B architecture, tokenizer, windows, and LoRA
protocol, with initialization as the only factor), pretrained initialization
reduces forecast MSE by $2.2\times$, $\sim 25\times$, and $\sim 6\times$ on
synthetic sine, ETTm1, and ETTh1.  We ask \emph{why}.  A linear probe on the
\emph{frozen} LLM classifies temporal dynamics (trend/periodic/local/mixture/
regime) at 96.3\% accuracy, while on the \emph{same windows} frozen numeric
generation parses only 26.7\% of forecasts and errs $5.5\times$ above the
oracle.  Language pretraining is therefore a temporal-structure prior: it helps
the LLM \emph{recognize} dynamics more than it makes it a better numeric
\emph{generator}.  As a frozen zero-shot expert router over lightweight
statistical experts, it matches the oracle on unseen dynamics 67.8\% of the
time (versus 12.8\% for hand features and 28.9\% for a random same-architecture
control) and cuts forecast error by 42\%.
\end{abstract}

\begin{keywords}
LLM, time-series forecasting, temporal dynamics, expert routing, zero-shot
\end{keywords}

\section{INTRODUCTION}
\label{sec:intro}
LLM-based time-series forecasting (LLM4TS) is an active area
\cite{timellm,autotimes,prdts}.  A central open question is \emph{why} language
pretraining should help predict numbers at all.  A prominent skeptical study
\cite{tan2024useful} found that removing or randomly initializing the LLM in
several LLM4TS methods does not hurt forecasting, concluding that gains come
from architecture (patching, attention) rather than language.  Recent
controlled comparisons report the opposite under parameter-efficient
fine-tuning of modern LLMs \cite{randominit,crossmodalm}.

This debate is confounded by uncontrolled factors: different methods freeze or
tune different components, use different tokenizers and prompts, and compare
against baselines of different capacity.  We contribute a controlled
measurement and a mechanistic hypothesis:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Controlled ablation} (Sec.~\ref{sec:e1}): with initialization as
  the only factor, pretrained Qwen3-8B beats random by $2.2\times$--$25\times$
  in MSE.
\item \textbf{Structure recognition without generation}
  (Sec.~\ref{sec:e2}--\ref{sec:e3}): a linear probe on the \emph{frozen} LLM
  classifies dynamics at 96.3\%; frozen numeric generation parses only 26.7\%
  of the same windows and errs $5.5\times$ above the oracle.
\item \textbf{Zero-shot expert router} (Sec.~\ref{sec:e4}): on unseen
  dynamics, the probe matches the oracle 67.8\% of the time versus 12.8\%
  (hand features) and 28.9\% (random same-architecture).
\item \textbf{Patch-level dynamic routing} (Sec.~\ref{sec:e6}): routing each
  horizon segment adapts to within-window dynamics changes ($-33\%$ MSE on
  regime-switch windows).
\end{itemize}

\section{CONTROLLED SETUP}
\label{sec:setup}
\textbf{Model and data.}  We use a local Qwen3-8B checkpoint (language
backbone only).  Each normalized history is rendered as ``History:
\texttt{+0.12 -0.04 \dots} Forecast:'' with two-decimal values clamped to
$[-9.99,9.99]$.  LoRA (rank 8) on $q/k/v/o$; teacher forcing with masked
prompt loss; free-running greedy evaluation; context 64, horizon 16; 256
train / 64 test windows; 5 epochs; train-only normalization.  Data: synthetic
sine and the $OT$ channel of ETTm1/ETTh1.
\textbf{Control.}  The random initialization instantiates the identical
architecture with random weights and the same LoRA adapters, windows, and
seed.
\textbf{Probe.}  For a frozen model, the last-layer hidden state of the final
prompt token feeds a linear (softmax) probe.  The feature floor uses nine
hand-crafted statistics (slope, linear $R^2$, volatility, lag-1
autocorrelation, dominant period/strength, spectral entropy/concentration,
range ratio).
\textbf{Dynamics.}  Five synthetic kinds: trend, periodic, local (AR(1)),
mixture, and regime (trend switching to periodic mid-window).  Experts fit per
window: linear extrapolation (trend), autocorrelation period detection +
seasonal-naive (periodic), and stable damped AR(5) (local).

\section{EXPERIMENTS}

\subsection{Language pretraining helps}\label{sec:e1}
Table~\ref{tab:forecast} aggregates three seeds.  Pretrained initialization
reduces MSE by $2.2\times$--$25\times$ with parse coverage $1.0$ and far lower
variance, and roughly halves spectral amplitude error.  This directly
contradicts \cite{tan2024useful} in the parameter-efficient fine-tuning regime
of a modern LLM.  To rule out that LoRA alone handicaps the random backbone, we
repeat the ETTm1 comparison on Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B with \emph{full} fine-tuning:
even with full capacity, random initialization stays $10$--$13\times$ worse than
pretrained (0.6B full: 0.141 vs.\ 0.012; 1.7B full: 0.142 vs.\ 0.015), so the
benefit is not a LoRA artifact.

\subsection{Recognize versus generate}\label{sec:e3}
On 40 windows stratified across the five dynamics, the frozen probe recognizes
the kind with 96.7\% accuracy; on the \emph{same} windows, frozen greedy
numeric generation parses only 26.7\% of forecasts and the complete ones have
MSE 1.85 --- $5.5\times$ the oracle expert error (0.33).  Numeric generation is
the fragile mode; recognition is robust.

To rule out that the generation failure is merely poor output \emph{formatting},
we repeat recognition and generation on the frozen Qwen3-0.6B with
\emph{grammar-constrained decoding}: at every step the logits are masked to the
exact expected character class of the ``$+d.dd$'' format, forcing 100\% parse
coverage.  On 120 windows, recognition reaches 98.3\%, while the
formatting-perfect generation still errs MSE 2.47 --- $6.2\times$ the oracle
expert error (0.40).  The recognize-versus-generate gap therefore persists even
after removing the formatting confound.

\subsection{Zero-shot frozen-LLM expert router}\label{sec:e4}
We train the router on three clean kinds and apply it to the two \emph{unseen}
kinds (mixture, regime).  Table~\ref{tab:router} compares hand-feature, frozen
pretrained-probe, and frozen random-probe routers against oracle, best-single,
and uniform baselines.  The pretrained probe matches the oracle 67.8\% and cuts
MSE by 42\% versus the feature router (0.739 vs.\ 1.278; soft routing 0.723);
the random control transfers far worse (28.9\%), isolating language pretraining
as the source of transfer.  We note honestly that best-single (trend) remains
competitive here because trend dominates the oracle; routing's clear advantage
is recognition transfer, not uniformly beating every expert.  As a non-LLM
learned control, a small MLP trained on the same three clean classes (raw
windows) transfers OOD at only 37.5\% oracle accuracy (MSE 0.80), far below the
frozen pretrained probe (67.8\%), so the transfer is not explained by any
supervised nonlinear classifier on the same data.

\textbf{Real-data validation.}  We apply the synthetic-trained routers to five
real datasets (ETTm1, ETTh1, electricity, weather, exchange\_rate; 300 windows
each, no adaptation).  The hand-feature router collapses into the \emph{local}
expert on three of five (ETTm1 MSE 0.657, ETTh1 0.748, exchange\_rate 1.591),
while the frozen pretrained probe avoids \emph{local} everywhere and reaches
MSE 0.020--0.374, beating the feature router on four of five datasets and
nearly matching the best single expert on four.  The random same-architecture
probe often degenerates to always-$\emph{periodic}$, which is near-optimal on
homogeneous series (ETTm1, ETTh1, weather, exchange\_rate); on electricity,
where \emph{local} is genuinely best, the LLM's local-avoidance costs a little
(0.374 vs.\ best-single 0.343).  The language-pretraining advantage is clearest
on diverse and \emph{unseen} dynamics (Sec.~\ref{sec:e4}); on a single
homogeneous real series a degenerate policy suffices.

\subsection{Patch-level dynamic routing}\label{sec:e6}
We split the horizon into four 4-step segments and route each segment
independently with the frozen probe on the 32 steps before it (probe trained on
32-step clean patches).  Dynamic routing helps exactly where dynamics change
within a window: MSE drops from 0.82 to 0.55 on regime windows (toward the
per-segment oracle 0.28) and from 1.64 to 1.31 on local windows; stationary
windows (periodic/trend) do not benefit because 32-step patches carry weaker
structural evidence.  The LLM patch router still beats the hand-feature patch
router by 34\% overall (0.755 vs.\ 1.144), confirming that the temporal-structure
prior is most valuable when dynamics are heterogeneous --- across windows or
within them.

\section{RELATION TO PRIOR WORK}
\label{sec:related}
LLM4TS methods reprogram or fine-tune LLMs \cite{timellm,autotimes,prdts}; the
skeptical line argues gains come from architecture rather than language
\cite{tan2024useful}.  Same-architecture initialization studies report
pretraining benefits under fine-tuning \cite{randominit,crossmodalm}.  LLM
as-router and MoE hybrids route tokens or tasks through experts
\cite{lego,fmllm,dmoe}; we route \emph{temporal dynamics} with a \emph{frozen}
LLM and measure zero-shot transfer to unseen dynamics.  Our spectral
diagnostics are a natural fit for ICASSP.

\section{CONCLUSION}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Under a strict controlled comparison, language pretraining is a
temporal-structure prior in LLM time-series forecasting: it drives large
forecast-error gains under LoRA fine-tuning, and a \emph{frozen} pretrained LLM
recognizes temporal dynamics and routes experts zero-shot far better than hand
features or a random same-architecture control, while numeric generation
remains fragile.  The structure prior is most valuable under heterogeneous
dynamics, whether across windows or within them.  Limitations: small window
counts and synthetic probe data (three seeds); future work extends dynamic
routing to longer horizons and real multivariate data.
